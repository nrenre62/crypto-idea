# Track: Foundations *(free)*
# Module 1 — How Markets Really Work

*Goal: understand why prices move the way they do, so the noise stops running your decisions.*

### Lesson 1 — Price is a story, not a scoreboard
**Hook.** A coin rips 40% in a week and you feel dumb for not being in, so you buy. Two weeks later you're down 30% and feel dumber. That whole ride had almost nothing to do with the project.
**The idea.** A price isn't a measure of what something is worth — it's the sum of what everyone *feels* it's worth right now. Fundamentals move slowly; emotions move fast. So price swings far more than reality does, and a rising price pulls in more buyers, which pushes it higher, until it doesn't.
**How to apply it.** When a price is screaming, ask one question: *did the project change, or did the mood change?* If only the mood moved, the price is telling you about other people's feelings — not about value.
**Takeaway.** Price tells you what the crowd feels today, not what a coin is worth.
**Apply:** Open your portfolio, pick the coin that's moved most this week, and write one sentence — did the *project* change, or just the price?

### Lesson 2 — Nothing goes in a straight line
**Hook.** Every cycle, someone says "this time it's different" right before it isn't. The ones who believe them buy the top; the ones who panic sell the bottom.
**The idea.** Markets move in cycles that overshoot both directions and snap back toward the middle. Greed pushes prices far above where they should be; fear drags them far below. The further it stretches one way, the harder it tends to swing back. Trees don't grow to the sky — and very few real things go all the way to zero.
**How to apply it.** When something feels euphoric and unstoppable, assume you're nearer the top than the start. When everyone's disgusted and quiet, assume the floor is closer than it feels. You won't nail the turn — just don't bet the current direction lasts forever.
**Takeaway.** Every extreme is temporary. Bet on reversion, not on the straight line.
**Apply:** Look at your worst-performing coin. Is it down because the thesis broke, or because the whole market is in a fear swing? Write which.

### Lesson 3 — The crowd is the trap
**Hook.** The hardest thing in this game isn't analysis. It's watching a friend get rich on a coin you passed on — and not caving.
**The idea.** Bull markets climb through three moods: first a few people quietly believe, then most people notice, then everyone is certain it goes up forever. That last mood — where price stops mattering and "you just have to be in" — is where the most money is lost. It's herd behavior: the pain of missing out overrides judgment, so people buy high and, later, sell low.
**How to apply it.** Use the crowd as a contrarian gauge. Universal excitement is a caution flag; universal disgust is a reason to look closer. When you feel FOMO pulling, that feeling is data — usually that you're late.
**Takeaway.** When everyone's sure, the easy money is already gone. Move against the mood.
**Apply:** Think of the last coin you bought because it was "everywhere." Write what mood you were in when you hit buy.

### Lesson 4 — A red candle is not risk
**Hook.** You held a good coin through a 50% drop and white-knuckled the whole way. You held a dead coin through the same drop telling yourself it'd come back. Same candle, completely different situations — and you treated them the same.
**The idea.** Volatility is not risk. A swing is just movement. Real risk is the money you never get back — the project that dies, the rug, the slow bleed to zero. A coin can be volatile and perfectly fine, or calm and quietly lethal. Judging risk by how much the price wiggles is how people panic-sell good coins and hold dead ones.
**How to apply it.** Every time a coin drops, split two questions: *is this just movement, or is the money actually at risk?* The first you can ride. The second you act on.
**Takeaway.** Risk isn't a red candle — it's not getting the money back.
**Apply:** Pick a coin that dropped hard recently. Label it "just volatile" or "actually at risk" — and say why.

### Lesson 5 — You can't predict, but you can read the room
**Hook.** Nobody — not the loudest analyst you follow — actually knows where the market goes next. The ones who sound certain are guessing with confidence.
**The idea.** Forecasting the market is a losing game. But reading where you *are* is not. Take the temperature two ways: the numbers (are valuations stretched or cheap?) and the mood (is money easy, is everyone euphoric, are new coins bought with zero questions?). That won't tell you what happens tomorrow — it tells you what's more and less likely, which is enough.
**How to apply it.** Stop trying to call the top or bottom. Ask instead, "is the room greedy or fearful right now?" and let that set how careful you are — not what you predict.
**Takeaway.** Don't forecast the weather. Just notice whether it's storming.
**Apply:** In one line, describe the market's mood today — greedy, fearful, or numb — and what that means for how careful you'll be.

### Lesson 6 — What you actually control
**Hook.** You can't control whether the market pumps tomorrow. You can't control whether your coin's team ships. You spent months stressing about all of it anyway.
**The idea.** The market does what it does — cycles, swings, moods, all outside your hands. Two things are inside your hands: the research you do before you buy, and how you react when the price moves. That's the whole game. Everyone obsessing over predictions is fighting the part they can't control and ignoring the part they can.
**How to apply it.** Pour your energy into the two things that are yours: check the project properly before you buy, and decide in advance how you'll react. The market's mood becomes background noise.
**Takeaway.** You can't control the market. You can control your research and your reaction. That's enough.
**Apply:** Write the one thing you'll commit to doing *before* your next buy — no matter how good the coin looks.
