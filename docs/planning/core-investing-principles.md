# Core Investing Principles
**v1 · original framing for in-app and published use**

> A standalone set of investing principles and how to apply them. No names, sources,
> titles, or proprietary terms — written in original language for free use in the app,
> the AI knowledge base, and published material.

## How to read this

Each entry is a principle plus a practical way to use it. The principles are universal;
the application notes assume someone researching an asset before buying and holding it
through the noise.

---

### 1. Earn your conviction
Conviction is what's left after you've done the work — not a feeling you start with.
Certainty without research is just hope.
**Use it:** Never buy on a feeling. Run your checklist first and let the result, not the
excitement, decide.

### 2. Hold the reason, not the price
You bought for a reason you wrote down, so you reconsider when that *reason* breaks — not
when the price moves.
**Use it:** Before buying, write the one reason you're buying and the one thing that
would prove you wrong. Revisit only when that specific thing happens.

### 3. Keep your own scorecard
Measure yourself against your own reasons, not the crowd's applause or whatever is
trending.
**Use it:** When you feel the pull to chase what everyone's buying, re-read your written
reasons. If they haven't changed, neither should you.

### 4. Look past the obvious
The consensus view is already in the price. An edge comes from seeing what others miss,
not from agreeing with them.
**Use it:** Ask not "is this good?" but "what does the crowd already believe, and what
might they be getting wrong?"

### 5. Real risk is losing the money for good
A price swing isn't risk; a permanent loss is. Something can be volatile and safe, or
calm and lethal.
**Use it:** Separate "this dropped" from "this is broken." Only the second is real risk.

### 6. The price is part of the risk
A great asset bought at the wrong price is still a bad investment. Your protection is the
gap between what it's worth and what you pay.
**Use it:** Even when something passes every quality check, ask whether the *price* still
leaves a cushion. Quality is not the same as a good buy.

### 7. Do less
Most damage is self-inflicted — through trading, switching, and reacting. Sitting still
is a discipline, not laziness.
**Use it:** Make "do nothing" your default. Act only when your reason changes or a clear
opportunity appears.

### 8. Let time compound
Gains arrive late and build on themselves; every unnecessary exit resets the clock.
**Use it:** Once you own a researched position, leave it alone. Don't trade around it.

### 9. Concentrate on a few you understand
A handful of positions you've researched deeply beats a pile of bets you can't explain.
**Use it:** Cap your list. If you can't keep up with all of them, you own too many.

### 10. Make the downside small and the upside large
Favor bets where being wrong costs little and being right pays a lot — and never one that
can ruin you.
**Use it:** Size positions so a total loss is survivable. The bigger the unknown, the
smaller the stake.

### 11. Prefer low risk with high uncertainty
Uncertainty scares the crowd and depresses the price; if the real downside is small, that
fear is your bargain.
**Use it:** Look for things that are *unclear* but not *fragile* — the discount lives in
the discomfort.

### 12. Move against the mood
Lean in when the crowd is fearful and quiet; pull back when it's euphoric and loud.
**Use it:** Treat universal excitement as a caution flag and widespread disgust as a
reason to look closer.

### 13. Read the moment, don't forecast
You can't predict where the market goes, but you can read where it stands — and that's
enough to tilt the odds.
**Use it:** Read the mood from valuations and crowd behavior. Judge what's *likely*, not
what's *certain*.

### 14. Expect cycles that overshoot
Excess builds in both directions and then reverses; success sows the seeds of failure,
and failure the seeds of recovery. Nothing rises forever, and little goes to zero.
**Use it:** When something "can only go up," assume you're late. When everyone has given
up, assume a floor is near.

### 15. Watch for a change in the rules
Beyond the normal up-and-down, the whole environment occasionally shifts. When the
underlying conditions change, the strategy that just worked can stop working.
**Use it:** Don't assume the last cycle's winners are this cycle's. Ask what's different
about the environment now.

### 16. Copy the method, not the tip
Borrowing a proven, disciplined *process* is smart. Copying someone's *pick* is just
following the crowd with extra steps.
**Use it:** Adopt the discipline; never buy something only because someone you admire
bought it.

### 17. Stay inside what you can explain
If you can't write down why something has value, you don't have a thesis — you have a
hope.
**Use it:** The test is the blank page. If you can't fill it, you don't understand it
well enough to own it.

### 18. Weigh the thing itself over the headlines
How an asset performs depends far more on what it actually is and who's building it than
on the macro noise around it.
**Use it:** Spend your attention on the fundamentals and the builders, not the daily
narrative.
