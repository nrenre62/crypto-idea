## The research funnel — 5 questions, in order

This is the full **method** every coin runs through. Most die in the first three. Don't
waste a second on revenue for a coin that isn't even being built.

### 1. Is it still being built? *(the fastest kill)*
- **Check:** GitHub — last commit date, whether the *core* repos are moving, how many people contribute.
- **Green / red:** real code in the main repos in the last week, multiple contributors — vs. silent 3+ months, or zero commits this year (effectively dead).
- **The trap:** a busy graph that's all README and docs edits. **Commits ≠ shipping.** Look at *what* changed, not just *that* something did.
- **Why first:** price follows building. A team that stops committing code has usually stopped shipping. The chart is the last to know.

### 2. Is the team still here?
- **Check:** has the founder appeared publicly in the last 3 months? Is this their mission or a side project?
- **The trap:** a loud marketing account in front of a silent founder.

### 3. Will I get diluted?
- **Check:** the unlock schedule — how much supply is locked, when it releases, who gets it.
- **The trap:** a coin that looks cheap by market cap but has several times more supply waiting to unlock.

### 4. Is the price move real?
- **Check:** volume across real venues vs. one source carrying it; on-chain activity rising with price.
- **The trap:** the same coins traded back and forth to manufacture excitement.

### 5. Is the yield real?
- **Check:** protocol revenue (people paying to use it) vs. emissions (printing new tokens to pay you).
- **Why last:** it only matters for survivors.

## What the app automates vs. what the method teaches

The app does **not** auto-score all five. It surfaces:

- **4 conviction signals**, generated and cached: **Dev** (step 1 — build health), **Founders** (step 2 — founder presence), **Team** (step 2 — focus/commitment), **Community** (sentiment).
- **A scam red-flag** — a flag, not a score — derived from the danger end of the other checks: anonymous/unverifiable team, wash-trade volume pattern, and rug-style tokenomics (insider-heavy supply, cliff dumps). *Inputs adjustable.*

Steps **3–5 (dilution, volume authenticity, real revenue)** are taught *here* and in the
Learn tab, and the user applies them with the framework. The funnel is the whole method;
the signals are the automated subset. The signals are shown **separately — there is no
combined conviction score**, because a single number reads like a buy rating, and the app
never rates a buy.

## When to reconsider — conviction ≠ stubbornness

You hold on the thesis you **wrote down**, not on price. So the trigger to reconsider
isn't "it dropped" — it's "the reason I wrote down stopped being true." That's why you
write it *before* you buy, while calm: you decide in advance what would change your mind.
This is *Hold the reason, not the price* and *Stay inside what you can explain* from the
principles.

**The dip test** *(the founding scar):* a coin drops 50%. Buying more is either the
smartest or the dumbest move, and they look identical. One question separates them: did
the **reason** change, or just the **price**? Thesis intact → opportunity. Thesis broken
→ averaging into a corpse. Run the funnel before you add.
