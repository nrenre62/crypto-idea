# How to research crypto

A methodology for judging a crypto asset before you buy it and holding it through the noise. It pairs a bottom-up research funnel — how to tell real development, real volume, and real yield from the fakes — with a set of timeless investing principles that govern price, risk, cycles, and the psychology of holding.

## Who this is for

A retail crypto investor who has been burned by hype and FOMO, still believes in the market, and wants a real framework instead of another price target. Not a trader chasing candles. Someone who wants to own the right assets and actually hold them.

## The one belief everything hangs on

Conviction beats timing. You do not win by calling the bottom — you win by holding the right assets through the noise, and by not holding the wrong ones out of stubbornness. Conviction is what is left over after you check the project, not a feeling you start with.

Conviction without research is just expensive hope. Earn it. The rest of this document is how.

## The research funnel — five questions, in order

Every asset runs through the same method. Most die in the first three, so ask them in order and stop early. Do not waste a second analyzing revenue for a project that is not even being built.

### 1. Is it still being built?

This is the fastest kill.

- Check the code repositories: last commit date, whether the core repos are moving, how many people contribute.
- Green: real code landing in the main repos recently, multiple independent contributors.
- Red: silence for months, or effectively zero meaningful commits this year.
- The trap: a busy activity graph that is all README and documentation edits. Commits are not the same as shipping — look at what changed, not just that something did.
- Why first: price follows building. A team that stops committing code has usually stopped shipping, and the chart is the last to know.

### 2. Is the team still here?

- Check whether the founders have appeared publicly and substantively in recent months, and whether this is their mission or a side project.
- The trap: a loud marketing account in front of a silent founder. Attention is cheap; sustained building is not.

### 3. Will I get diluted?

- Check the token unlock schedule: how much supply is locked, when it releases, and who receives it.
- The trap: an asset that looks cheap by circulating market cap but has several times more supply waiting to unlock. Fully-diluted value, not the headline market cap, is the real size.
- Watch for cliff unlocks that hand large allocations to insiders on a single date — that is a scheduled sell wall.

### 4. Is the price move real?

- Check volume across multiple independent venues rather than one exchange carrying the whole print, and look for on-chain activity rising alongside price.
- The trap: wash trading — the same coins traded back and forth to manufacture the appearance of demand. Real usage leaves on-chain footprints; wash volume does not.

### 5. Is the yield real?

- Check whether returns come from protocol revenue — people actually paying to use the thing — or from emissions, which is printing new tokens to pay you with dilution.
- Why last: yield only matters for the survivors that cleared the first four questions. A high advertised APR funded entirely by emissions is a countdown, not an income stream.

## What a checklist can automate versus what you apply yourself

Some of the funnel can be surfaced as signals; some of it stays a judgment you make. A useful split:

- Signals that lend themselves to automation: build health (question 1), founder presence and team focus (question 2), and community sentiment. These are shown separately, never fused into a single combined score — one number reads like a buy rating, and the method never rates a buy.
- A scam red flag rather than a score: derived from the danger end of the checks — an anonymous or unverifiable team, a wash-trade volume pattern, and rug-style tokenomics such as insider-heavy supply or cliff dumps.
- Judgments you apply yourself: dilution, volume authenticity, and real revenue (questions 3 through 5). These reward reading the actual schedules and on-chain data with the framework in hand.

The funnel is the whole method; any automated signals are only a subset of it.

## When to reconsider — conviction is not stubbornness

You hold on the thesis you wrote down, not on the price. So the trigger to reconsider is never "it dropped" — it is "the reason I wrote down stopped being true." That is why you write the thesis before you buy, while calm: you decide in advance what would change your mind.

The dip test makes this concrete. An asset drops 50%. Buying more is either the smartest or the dumbest move, and the two look identical in the moment. One question separates them: did the reason change, or just the price?

- Thesis intact, only the price moved: this is volatility, and possibly an opportunity. Run the funnel again before you add.
- Thesis broken: this is risk, and averaging down is averaging into a corpse. Exit and log what you missed.

## The principles underneath

The funnel tells you whether a specific asset is any good. These principles supply what a bottom-up checklist alone cannot: awareness of the whole market's cycle, the psychology of holding, and the discipline of price and position size. Each is stated as general investing wisdom and paired with a way to use it.

### Earn your conviction

Conviction is what remains after the work, not a feeling you start with. Certainty without research is hope.

Use it: never buy on a feeling. Run the funnel first and let the result, not the excitement, decide.

### Hold the reason, not the price

You bought for a reason you wrote down, so you reconsider when that reason breaks — not when the price moves.

Use it: before buying, write the one reason you are buying and the one thing that would prove you wrong. Revisit only when that specific thing happens.

### Keep your own scorecard

Measure yourself against your own reasons, not the crowd's applause or whatever is trending. FOMO is the crowd's scorecard running your decisions — you buy because everyone else is up, not because you have a reason.

Use it: when you feel the pull to chase what everyone is buying, re-read your written reasons. If they have not changed, neither should you.

### Look past the obvious

The consensus view is already in the price. An edge comes from second-level thinking — seeing what others miss — not from agreeing with them. To beat the average you have to diverge from the crowd and be correct.

Use it: ask not "is this good?" but "what does the crowd already believe, and what might they be getting wrong?" If every influencer has already called it, the easy money is gone.

### Real risk is losing the money for good

A price swing is not risk; a permanent loss is. Something can be volatile and safe, or calm and lethal. Risk is largely hidden and unquantifiable, which is exactly why it gets underpriced.

Use it: separate "this dropped" from "this is broken." Only the second is real risk. This is the dip test made formal.

### The price is part of the risk

A great asset bought at the wrong price is still a bad investment. Your protection is the margin of safety — the gap between what something is worth and what you pay. Risk comes less from weak fundamentals than from too high a price.

Use it: even when something passes every quality check, ask whether the price still leaves a cushion. Quality is not the same as a good buy.

### Avoiding losers beats chasing winners

For almost everyone, defense is the surer path — you rarely have the skill to both dodge every disaster and catch every moonshot. You do not need the 100x; you need to not get wiped out.

Use it: put survival first. One avoided rug is worth more than one caught moonshot. Cut the disasters and let the survivors compound.

### Make the downside small and the upside large

Favor bets where being wrong costs little and being right pays a lot — heads I win, tails I do not lose much — and never one that can ruin you.

Use it: size positions so a total loss is survivable. The bigger the unknown, the smaller the stake. A wrong call should be an annoyance, not a catastrophe.

### Prefer low risk with high uncertainty

Uncertainty scares the crowd and depresses the price. If the real downside is small, that fear is your bargain.

Use it: look for things that are unclear but not fragile — the discount lives in the discomfort, not in genuine danger of going to zero.

### Concentrate on a few you understand

A handful of positions you have researched deeply beats a pile of bets you cannot explain. Thirty lottery tickets is not a portfolio; it is a casino floor.

Use it: cap your list. Concentrate on the few you have researched to the bone. If you cannot keep up with all of them, you own too many.

### Do less

Most damage is self-inflicted — through trading, switching, and reacting. Sitting still is a discipline, not laziness. Activity is the enemy of returns.

Use it: make "do nothing" your default. Act only when your reason changes or a clear opportunity appears.

### Let time compound

Gains arrive late and build on themselves; every unnecessary exit resets the clock and pays the fees. Compounding is back-end heavy, which is exactly why people quit before it shows up.

Use it: once you own a researched position, leave it alone. Do not trade around it.

### Let your winners run

The mistake is not only panic-selling losers — it is selling winners too early because you are up and it feels safe to lock it in. A doubling is not a thesis-breaker.

Use it: reconsider only on a thesis break, and let that cut both ways. Price moving up is no more a sell signal than price moving down is a buy signal.

### Move against the mood

Lean in when the crowd is fearful and quiet; pull back when it is euphoric and loud. Excess builds in both directions and then reverses.

Use it: treat universal excitement as a caution flag and widespread disgust as a reason to look closer. When something "can only go up," assume you are late.

### Read the moment, do not forecast

You cannot predict where the market goes, but you can read where it stands — and reading the temperature is enough to tilt the odds. Use both hard valuation gauges and the qualitative mood of the room.

Use it: judge what is likely, not what is certain. A bull market climbs from a few perceptive buyers, to the broad crowd, to the final conviction that it rises forever — and that last stage is the top.

### Expect cycles that overshoot

Markets oscillate around an upward trend, and psychology pushes price far above and far below it before reverting. One event causes the next: success plants the seeds of failure, and failure plants the seeds of recovery. Trees do not grow to the sky, and few things go to zero.

Use it: when euphoria says "no price is too high," assume you are late. When everyone has given up, assume a floor is near.

### Watch for a change in the rules

Beyond the normal up-and-down of cycles, the whole environment occasionally shifts on a structural level. When the underlying regime changes — liquidity, rates, the dominant narrative — the strategy that just made everyone rich can quietly stop working.

Use it: do not assume the last cycle's winners are this cycle's. Ask what is genuinely different about the environment now, and do not fight the last war.

### Copy the method, not the tip

Borrowing a proven, disciplined process is smart. Copying someone's pick is just following the crowd with extra steps.

Use it: adopt the discipline; never buy something only because someone you admire bought it. Cloning the process is wisdom. Copying the crowd's positions is FOMO with extra steps.

### Stay inside what you can explain

Circle of competence: if you cannot write down why something has value, you do not have a thesis — you have a hope.

Use it: the test is the blank page. If you cannot fill it, you do not understand it well enough to own it.

### Study your own mistakes ruthlessly

Your broken theses are your best teacher. Keep a brutal record of where you were wrong and why.

Use it: when a thesis breaks, do not just exit — read back what you wrote and learn the tell you missed. Every reconsideration is a lesson logged.

### Weigh the thing itself over the headlines

How an asset performs depends far more on what it actually is and who is building it than on the macro noise around it. Micro trumps macro.

Use it: spend your attention on the fundamentals and the builders — the bottom-up funnel above — not the daily narrative.

## What this framework is NOT

- Not buy or sell advice. It tells you what is true so you can decide for yourself.
- Not price prediction. No targets, no "10x."
- Not a replacement for judgment. The decision is always yours.
