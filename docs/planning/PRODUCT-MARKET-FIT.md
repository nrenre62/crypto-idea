# Product-Market Fit Framework

> You don't find product-market fit by building. You find it by understanding a
> painful problem so deeply that the solution becomes obvious. Most apps fail
> because they're solutions looking for a problem. Think the opposite way.

---

## The Core Truth

People don't buy products. They "hire" them to do a job. Existing alternatives
are **proof of demand**, not competition to fear. If nobody currently pays time,
money, or effort to solve a problem, it's probably not a real problem.

---

## Step 1 — Find a Problem People Already Pay to Solve

A real problem has these fingerprints:

- People already spend **money** on it (tools, subscriptions)
- People already spend **time** on it (hours, manual work)
- People already spend **effort** on it (frustrating workflows)
- There are many **bad alternatives** (spreadsheets, multiple tools)
- People **complain** about it openly

---

## Step 2 — The "Job to Be Done" Lens

Ask:

- What job is the customer trying to get done?
- What are they "firing" to hire you?
- What does success look like for THEM?

**Example real jobs (crypto):**

| Pain | Currently solved with |
|------|----------------------|
| Funds across 6 exchanges + 3 wallets, no idea of true portfolio value | Messy spreadsheets |
| 2,000 transactions, no idea what tax is owed | Paying $500 to an accountant |
| Missing news that moves coins, can't watch 24/7 | Anxiety and FOMO |

Each has: real pain, real money/time spent, bad current solutions.

---

## Step 3 — Talk to Humans BEFORE Building

90% of founders build first, ask later. Do the reverse.

Find 20 people who have this problem. Ask them (never pitch, only listen):

1. "Walk me through the last time you dealt with X"
2. "What tools do you use now? What do you hate about them?"
3. "How much time/money does this cost you?"
4. "What have you tried that didn't work?"
5. "If you had a magic wand, what would you change?"

**The golden signal:** *"I would pay for that right now"* — or better,
*"why doesn't this already exist?"*

---

## Step 4 — The Value Must Be Obvious in Seconds

Complete this sentence so a stranger gets it instantly:

> "[Product] helps [specific person] do [specific job] in [time] instead of [old painful way]"

**Example:**

> "CryptoIdea shows day traders their true P&L across all exchanges in one
> screen, instead of rebuilding spreadsheets every morning."

If you can't say it in one sentence, the value isn't clear enough yet.

---

## Step 5 — Charge Money Early

The only real proof of value is someone paying. Free users lie. Paying users
tell the truth.

Pre-sell before fully building:

- "I'm building X. It'll cost $20/month. Want early access?"
- If they pay → real
- If they say "maybe later" → not real yet

---

## Step 6 — The Painful-Enough Test

The problem must be:

- **FREQUENT** — happens often (daily/weekly, not yearly)
- **PAINFUL** — genuinely frustrating, not mild
- **EXPENSIVE** — costs real time/money currently
- **URGENT** — they want it solved NOW

A vitamin (nice to have) won't sell. A painkiller (must have) will.
**Build painkillers.**

---

## The Action Plan

1. Pick ONE specific painful job (not "crypto app" — too broad)
2. Find 20 people who do that job today (Twitter, Discord, Reddit)
3. Interview them — listen for pain, money, time wasted
4. Find the ONE problem they all complain about
5. Describe the solution in one sentence
6. Pre-sell it before building
7. Build the smallest thing that solves that ONE job
8. Get 10 people paying
9. THEN expand

---

## The Hard Truth About Feature Lists

A product that does 4 things (prices, ideas, portfolio, news) is a feature list,
not a product. The strongest products do ONE job extraordinarily well first.

**Ask: Which ONE feature solves the most painful, frequent, expensive problem?**
Build that one until people pay. Add the rest later.
