## What these add to your framework

Your research funnel is **bottom-up** — is *this coin* any good? These investors add
the things it doesn't have:

1. **Top-down cycle awareness** (Marks) — where the whole market sits between greed and fear. This is what stops you buying the top.
2. **The psychology of holding** (all of them) — why we panic-sell and FOMO-buy, and how the discipline beats the instinct.
3. **Price discipline + asymmetry** (Marks, Pabrai) — the price you pay and how you structure the bet, not just *which* coin you pick.

That's the "market cycle," "psychology," and "sizing" gaps, filled by the people who wrote the book on them.

## The principles

### 1. Inner scorecard, not outer scorecard — *Buffett, via Pabrai*
Judge yourself by your own standards, not the crowd's applause.
- **In crypto:** FOMO *is* the outer scorecard running your decisions — you buy because everyone else is up, not because you have a reason. The crowd's portfolio becomes your scorecard, and you lose.
- **Your framework:** the journal *is* your inner scorecard. Your written thesis is the standard you answer to — not what's trending.
- **Feeds:** Journal premise · Module 4 (Conviction) · your #1 scar (bought the top on FOMO)

### 2. You can't predict — you can prepare — *Marks*
Nobody calls the top or the bottom. But you can read the *temperature*: euphoria ("this time is different," everyone's a genius) versus despair (nobody wants to hear the word crypto).
- **In crypto:** when your barber is shilling coins, the market is hot — be careful. When everyone's disgusted and quiet, that's usually when it pays to be brave.
- **Your framework:** this is "conviction beats timing" from the top down. You don't time it; you position for the odds and hold.
- **Feeds:** Module 1 (How Markets Work) · the cycle gap · scar: bought the top

### 3. Risk is permanent loss — not a red candle — *Marks*
Volatility isn't risk. Risk is the money you never get back.
- **In crypto:** a coin down 50% with an intact thesis is *volatility*. A dead project, a rug, a slow bleed to zero — that's *risk*. Learn the difference and you stop panic-selling the first and start avoiding the second.
- **Your framework:** this is the dip test made formal — did the *reason* change, or just the price? And it's why survival (sizing, custody, scam-spotting) comes first.
- **Feeds:** Module 3 (Portfolio Construction) · the sizing/custody/scam gaps · scar: held a dead coin too long

### 4. Avoiding losers beats chasing winners — *Marks*
In his memo on the choice between "fewer losers or more winners," Marks's point is that for almost everyone, *defense* is the surer path. You rarely have the skill to do both.
- **In crypto:** you don't need the 100x. You need to not get wiped out. Cut the disasters and the survivors compound.
- **Your framework:** survival first. One avoided rug is worth more than one caught moonshot.
- **Feeds:** Module 3 · the scam/custody gaps

### 5. Few bets, big bets, infrequent bets — *Pabrai · Viswanathan*
A handful of high-conviction positions you actually understand beats thirty you don't.
- **In crypto:** thirty lottery tickets isn't a portfolio, it's a casino floor. Concentrate on the few you've researched to the bone.
- **Your framework:** one winner per category. Concentration *is* the strategy.
- **Feeds:** Module 3 · your one-winner-per-category rule

### 6. Let your winners run — *Pabrai*
The mistake isn't only panic-selling losers — it's selling winners too early because you're up and it feels safe to "lock it in."
- **In crypto:** a 2x is not a thesis-breaker. If the reason you bought is *more* true now, being up is not a reason to sell.
- **Your framework:** reconsider on thesis-break — and that cuts both ways. Price moving up is no more a sell signal than price moving down is.
- **Feeds:** Module 4 · scar: panic-sold a good coin

### 7. Own the few real ones — then do nothing — *Pabrai*
Pabrai talks about a business he intends to own forever — once you own something genuinely great at a fair price, the work is done. Activity is the enemy of returns.
- **In crypto:** the goal isn't to trade. It's to find the handful that are real and hold them while everyone else churns their account to zero in fees and bad timing.
- **Your framework:** conviction beats timing; the hardest skill is sitting still.
- **Feeds:** Module 4 · Module 1

### 8. Patience compounds — the proof is boring — *Viswanathan*
He tells the story of his father: one reluctant ~$400 stake in a bank IPO in the early '90s, never touched, that grew into something life-changing over decades. The return came from *time and inaction*, not cleverness.
- **In crypto:** the boring hold beats the brilliant trade almost every time. Most damage is self-inflicted — by doing something.
- **Your framework:** the antidote to overtrading; the long game the journal protects.
- **Feeds:** Module 1 · Module 4

### 9. Study your own mistakes ruthlessly — *Pabrai*
Great investors keep a brutal record of where they were wrong and *why*.
- **In crypto:** your broken theses are your best teacher. When a thesis breaks, don't just exit — read back what you wrote and learn the tell you missed.
- **Your framework:** the journal's review loop. Every reconsider is a lesson logged.
- **Feeds:** Journal review · Module 4

### 10. Being right isn't enough — be right when the crowd is wrong — *Marks*
Second-level thinking: the obvious, crowded trade has no edge left in it. You profit by understanding the odds better than the consensus.
- **In crypto:** if every influencer has already called it, the easy money is gone. The edge is in the under-covered, well-built thing you found first.
- **Your framework:** the coverage-gap signal — low coverage can mean undiscovered, not worthless.
- **Feeds:** Module 2 (Reading Fundamentals) · Module 5

### 11. Heads I win, tails I don't lose much — *Pabrai (Dhandho)*
Structure every bet so the downside is small and the upside is large. Pabrai's whole Dhandho approach — and the immigrant motel-owners he writes about — is built on positions where being wrong costs little but being right pays for years.
- **In crypto:** size and select so a wrong call is survivable (a small position in something that can't take you to zero) while a right one can actually move the portfolio. Never take a bet that can ruin you, no matter how good the story.
- **Your framework:** survival first, turned into bet construction. It's the sizing principle and the dip test working together.
- **Feeds:** Module 3 · the sizing gap · scar: held a dead coin too long

### 12. Only buy bargains — the price you pay is the risk — *Marks · Pabrai*
Marks looks for one thing above all: a gap between price and value. A wonderful asset bought too expensively is still a bad investment.
- **In crypto:** quality is not the same as a good buy. The best coin in the world bought at the euphoric top is still a way to lose money. What protects you is the cushion between what it's worth and what you paid.
- **Your framework:** the missing half of the funnel — it tells you a coin is *good*, not that it's a good *buy right now*. The direct answer to buying the top.
- **Feeds:** Module 2 · Module 3 · scar: bought the top on FOMO

### 13. Clone the process — never the crowd — *Pabrai*
Pabrai built a fortune by shamelessly copying proven investors. But what he clones is their *method and discipline* — not a hot tip.
- **In crypto:** you don't need to be a genius. Copy the discipline of the people who've already won — which is exactly what this app teaches. Cloning the *process* is wisdom. Copying the crowd's *picks* is just FOMO with extra steps.
- **Your framework:** the inner scorecard again — borrow the method, never let the crowd's positions become your scorecard.
- **Feeds:** Module 5 · Module 1 · scar: chased an influencer's call

### 14. A sea change isn't a cycle — *Marks*
Beyond the up-and-down of cycles, the whole environment occasionally shifts on a structural level — Marks's "sea change." When the regime turns (rates, liquidity, the dominant narrative), the strategy that just made everyone rich quietly stops working.
- **In crypto:** the rules of the last bull market don't carry into the next one. What worked when everything pumped on free money fails when the regime changes. Notice the regime; don't fight the last war.
- **Your framework:** a layer above cycle-temperature — not just *where* in the cycle, but *which game* you're even playing.
- **Feeds:** Module 1 · the cycle gap

### 15. Compounding is the whole game — *Pabrai*
Time plus a great position, left undisturbed, is exponential. The surest way to kill it is to keep interrupting it — selling, switching, churning.
- **In crypto:** the money is made by holding the few real ones for a long time, not by trading around them. Every unnecessary exit resets the clock and pays the fees.
- **Your framework:** the engine under "let winners run" and "do nothing" — the math that makes patience actually pay.
- **Feeds:** Module 4 · Module 1

### 16. Know what you don't know — *Marks · Pabrai (via Munger)*
The most dangerous thing in markets is false confidence. Both come back to the same humility: stay inside what you actually understand, and respect how many ways your own mind can fool you (Munger's catalog of biases).
- **In crypto:** if you can't explain why a coin has value, you don't have a thesis — you have a hope. The discipline of writing it down forces the honesty.
- **Your framework:** the journal is the test — if you can't write the two answers, you're outside your competence. The cure for the dead-coin-with-conviction scar.
- **Feeds:** Journal premise · Module 4 · scar: bought a dead coin without checking
